Read this before you trust a number. A single camera cannot validly capture all three planes of motion at once — that is a geometry problem, not a coding one. Whichever plane faces the lens reads as a real angle; the other two are reconstructed from the pose model's depth estimate, which is noisy, especially for the small rotations seen at the hip and knee during gait. Treat this as a teaching and screening demo, not a clinical goniometer or a substitute for 3D motion capture / instrumented gait analysis.

How the angle is computed: for the selected joint, the three landmarks either side of it (e.g. hip, knee, ankle) are projected onto each anatomical plane and the included angle is measured, then reported as deviation from anatomical neutral (180° for hip/knee, 90° for ankle). This mirrors the logic of marker based motion analysis in principle, but without calibration, multi-camera triangulation, or a Grood–Suntay joint coordinate system, so it is a simplified approximation, not a validated measurement. All processing happens locally in your browser — no video or angle data is sent anywhere.
